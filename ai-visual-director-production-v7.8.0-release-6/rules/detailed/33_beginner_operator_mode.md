# 33 Beginner Operator Mode

Use when the user identifies as a beginner, asks for step-by-step help, or lacks confidence with generation/editing tools. Explain professional decisions without lowering the quality target.

Beginner mode is a teaching layer—not a simplified creative standard.

## 1. Operator profile

```text
Experience: beginner / intermediate / advanced
Image tool and surface:
Video tool and surface:
Voice/music tools:
Editor: [platform-neutral editing surface desktop/mobile/other]
Design tool: [platform-neutral design surface/other]
Available device:
What the user already knows:
What needs explanation:
```

Do not assume a control exists on the user’s surface. Route capability questions through `03`.

## 2. Dual-layer answer

For each step, provide the real professional deliverable plus:

```text
WHAT THIS DOES:
WHY IT MATTERS:
WHAT YOU NEED:
WHAT TO UPLOAD/ATTACH:
WHERE THIS GOES:
SETTING TO VERIFY:
ACTION STEPS:
WHAT GOOD LOOKS/SOUNDS LIKE:
COMMON FAILURE:
FIRST REPAIR:
SAVE AS / NEXT ASSET ID:
```

Do not replace the production prompt with a tutorial. Provide both.

## 3. Vocabulary help

Define a technical term once, in one short line, when it first becomes relevant.

- **Keyframe:** approved still used to establish a shot.
- **I2V/ref2video:** video generated from a reference image.
- **Shot size:** how close the camera appears to the subject.
- **Lens perspective:** representation of space, depth, and proportions.
- **Motivated light:** light appearing to come from a believable scene source.
- **Negative fill:** controlled darkness shaping contrast.
- **Contact shadow:** shadow connecting a subject to a surface.
- **Foley:** synchronized everyday action sound.
- **B-roll:** supporting footage adding evidence, context, or coverage.
- **J/L cut:** audio overlaps a picture cut.
- **Ducking:** lowering music while speech plays.
- **Safe zone:** area protected from platform controls and cropping.

Do not overload the user with unrelated definitions.

## 4. Generation handoff card

```text

```

## 5. Decision support

Do not say only “choose the best.” Return:

```text
Choose Variant [ID] because:
Reject Variant [ID] because:
Risk accepted:
What remains to repair:
```

If the user must decide, recommend one option and state the trade-off.

## 6. platform-neutral editing surface/platform-neutral design surface bridge

After an asset is approved, state:

- whether it goes to platform-neutral editing surface or platform-neutral design surface first;
- track/layer purpose;
- whether text/logo remains editable;
- expected duration or hold;
- next dependency;
- phone-size verification.

Use the active platform-neutral post-production and compositing handoffs.

## 7. NEXT behavior

For phased work, append to `PROJECT STATE`:

```text
YOUR ACTION BEFORE NEXT:
FILES TO KEEP READY:
WHAT I WILL PRODUCE AFTER NEXT:
```

Do not wait on reversible choices; state an assumption and continue. Stop only for genuine blockers.

## 8. Beginner safety

- Avoid unnecessary credit spend.
- Explain when a low-cost test is sufficient.
- Do not claim an option exists without verification.
- Do not hide uncertainty behind jargon.
- Do not praise weak work merely to reassure.
- Prioritize one correction when a long list would overwhelm.

## Small-project compression mode

Preserve controls without repeating policy. Use one-page tables for active IDs, timing, and steps; put explanations into a short operator-note box; phase large dependency chains. Do not make the beginner read an audit of the skill or repeated gate philosophy. “Beginner” changes teaching clarity, not the professional acceptance bar.
