# 31 Commercial Excellence Autopilot

Use automatically for every Production Sequence or Campaign/Long Project whose goal is film-level, commercial-level, premium brand, portfolio, or client work. For a Quick Shot, apply only the relevant checks without forcing the full process.

This system enforces ambition and rejects generic work. It improves the probability of exceptional output but does not claim guaranteed virality, awards, perfect generation, or legal clearance.

## Gate 1 — Quality target

When references exist or the quality bar is unclear, run `32_visual_taste_and_reference_calibration.md` before approving the target. Declare before concept work:

```text
Quality target: [film / commercial / premium social / documentary / animation / design]
Audience promise:
Desired start emotion → end emotion:
What must be remembered after viewing:
What action should follow:
Reference quality bar (category, not a copied campaign):
```

## Gate 2 — Automatic concept divergence

Generate at least three mechanically different territories using `29`:

1. Evidence
2. Tension
3. World

Every territory must include:

```text
Concept ID and one-line promise:
One-frame visual thesis:
Story turn and payoff:
Memory device (image/object/phrase/gesture/color/sound):
Sound identity:
Audience emotion:
Category clichés rejected:
Why it belongs only to this brief:
Production control path:
Rights/claim risks:
```

### Automatic rejection

Reject a territory and replace it before storyboarding when any is true:

- it could belong to another brand, creator, product, or film with only a name change;
- it is primarily a look, grade, lens, or effect rather than an idea;
- it relies on stock AI imagery, category clichés, or empty spectacle;
- the opening has no specific evidence, tension, objective, or question;
- the payoff does not fulfill the promise;
- the memory device or sound identity is absent;
- production risk exceeds the available references, tools, budget, or continuity plan;
- it copies a recognizable campaign, character, artist, or protected style.

Continue generating directions until at least two pass; recommend one and explain the trade-off.

## Gate 2A — Story logic and causality

Every narrative concept must declare:

```text
Character objective:
Problem:
Cause:
Visible evidence:
Character decision:
Consequence:
Permanent resolution:
Emotional change:
Lesson shown through action:
World/magic rule required:
Unresolved contradiction:
```

Automatically reject and replace the story logic when:

- the resolution removes the thing currently solving the problem;
- a character action has no causal effect;
- the ending contradicts an earlier rule or state;
- a magical event occurs without an understandable world rule;
- the moral is spoken but not demonstrated through choice and consequence;
- the character makes no meaningful decision or emotional change;
- the payoff contradicts, ignores, or fails to resolve the setup.

Do not proceed to storyboarding with an unresolved causal contradiction.

## Gate 3 — Anti-generic language conversion

Flag these words when they appear without a physical or editorial definition:

```text
cinematic | epic | premium | engaging | dynamic | dramatic | stunning | beautiful |
futuristic | emotional | high quality | professional | viral | luxury | powerful | cool
```

Convert every flagged word into observable decisions:

```text
Vague phrase:
Intended viewer effect:
Visible decision (composition/action/production design):
Optical decision (shot size/perspective/movement):
Light/material decision:
Sound/edit decision:
```

Example:

```text
Vague: dramatic premium lighting
Directed: one narrow hard key from camera-left skims brushed metal; negative fill holds the far side near black; a controlled highlight travels across the engraved edge; no second source.
```

## Gate 4 — Film-language board

Every important shot must have:

- story function;
- shot size and perspective;
- subject objective, action, or change;
- screen direction/eyeline when continuity applies;
- camera intention and endpoint;
- entry and exit frame;
- cut motivation;
- sound cue;
- continuity risk and fallback coverage.

Reject decorative shots that add no new information, emotion, proof, reaction, scale, rhythm, transition, or payoff.

## Gate 5 — Commercial control

Before expensive generation, lock what applies:

```text
Product/character/brand identity:
Geometry, materials, wardrobe, and world continuity:
Approved references and rights:
Palette, typography, logo, CTA, and claim language:
Deliverable ratios and post-only layers:
Approval gate, budget stop-loss, and regeneration limit:
```

## Gate 6 — Mode-specific craft

### Mode A — Photoreal/cinema

Require coherent perspective, motivated light, contact and weight, material behavior, controlled reflections, stable anatomy/geometry, intentional production design, and cross-shot color/exposure logic.

### Mode B — Animation

Require stable family, silhouette, proportion, line/shading, palette, readable staging, anticipation/action/reaction/hold, and no accidental style conversion.

### Mode C — Motion graphics

Require one meaning per scene, exact editable text/data, hierarchy, grid, consistent icon/type language, designed easing/holds, and logical transitions.

### Mode D — Hybrid

Require layer ownership, perspective/light/scale match, occlusion/mattes, contact shadow/reflection, edge/grain/blur integration, and a transition into and out of the hybrid state.

## Gate 7 — Sound identity

For every sequence, define:

```text
Dialogue/VO role:
Signature Foley or material sound:
Ambient world:
Music state and emotional arc:
Silence point:
Transition cue:
Mix priority:
```

Do not accept “emotional music” or “cinematic sound” without a cue-level plan.

## Gate 8 — No-ship decision

Do not recommend shipping while any blocking condition remains:

- concept is interchangeable or cliché-led;
- opening promise is unclear or unpaid;
- product/character/brand identity visibly fails;
- story has no turn, reaction, proof, or payoff;
- continuity defects remain visible at delivery size;
- sound is disconnected, masks speech, or lacks spatial continuity;
- critical typography/data/claims are inaccurate or baked beyond control;
- rights, consent, disclosure, or substantiation is unresolved;
- final edit feels like unrelated generated clips;
- required delivery/accessibility checks are incomplete.

Return one of:

- **PASS TO NEXT GATE**
- **REVISE — named variable and acceptance condition**
- **BLOCK — rights/claim/capability dependency**
- **NO-SHIP — regenerate, restructure, or discard**

## Automatic phase behavior

For phased work under `28`, run the relevant gate before ending each phase:

```text
P0: operator/tool profile + quality target + rights/capability blockers
P1: taste/reference calibration + concept divergence + story-logic/causality gate + automatic rejection + memory/sound identity
P2: film-language board
P3: commercial control + mode craft for stills/assets
P4: mode craft + continuity + coverage for motion
P5: sound identity + post/edit control
P6: evidence-based QA + no-ship decision
```

Record the gate result in `PROJECT STATE`. Do not silently proceed after a failed or blocked gate.

## Binding release controller

These excellence gates provide creative depth; `35_reliability_gate_controller.md` owns dependency freezes, receipts, output lint, and release authorization. A prose discussion of a gate is not a PASS. Record evidence in the `35` receipt before advancing.

## Durable-resolution proof

A future routine, repeated care, safety mechanism, or transformed state cannot pass through narration alone. Show it in the board, establish it as a credible world/system rule, or mark the ending unresolved. Ask: **What keeps the solved state solved when the final action, temporary effect, or helper stops?** The active storyboard—not a correction note—must contain the approved resolution.
