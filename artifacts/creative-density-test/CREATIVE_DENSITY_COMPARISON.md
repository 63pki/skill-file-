## Part A — raw chat mode

*One unstructured prompt paragraph; no skill fields are used inside it.*

> Before the roastery door is unlocked, the room still belongs to the dark: a matte-black kraft coffee bag stands on a scarred oak tasting bench, left of the frame's quiet center, its copper zipper catching one hairline of first sunlight like a coal that has not gone out. Low window light skims the paper's dry tooth so the black stays tactile, not plastic, and then falls away across the empty right half into a deep, clean shadow where a website headline can breathe. One espresso cup has just been set down after the first pull; a dark crescent clings to its lip, a ring is sinking into the wood, and a few grounds sit where they escaped—not sprinkled, not arranged. No hands, only the warmth of someone recently here. The room feels pre-opening: dust in the beam, cool quiet beyond it, a breath before the day begins. Keep the bag crisp and true, front panel unlettered rather than inventing a brand mark; the cup can soften just at the edge of focus. Let copper glow once, let the kraft remain matte, and let the long shadow carry the composition to the copy-safe right. 16:9 website hero, near-counter camera, premium without showroom sterility, tactile, hushed, expectant. No second package, decorative bean scatter, fake writing, claims, oversized steam or atmospheric fog.

### 1. Which is more creatively alive?

**Raw wins at prompt level.** Specifically, it makes the setting feel time-bound and inhabited: “the room still belongs to the dark,” the zipper as “one hairline of first sunlight,” grounds “not sprinkled, not arranged,” and a person recently gone. Those details have causes; they are not generic “cinematic/premium” adjectives. The skill version is not dead—it has a real moment and one human trace—but its final paste block reads more like an engineered brief than a voice. That loss is mostly my drafting choice, not a hard prohibition in the skill.

### 2. What did raw generate that skill could not?

**Nothing the skill could not express.** Raw found a more memorable cadence and a compact sensory image. The skill could carry every one of those visual facts; I did not preserve enough of their phrasing when translating the card into its labelled envelope. The selected raw image also leaves a slightly calmer right-hand field, but that cannot be credited to the prompt alone because the image candidates came from different models.

### 3. What did skill mode have that raw lacked?

The skill made the source of truth explicit: only black kraft and copper zip are known; no package reference, logo, origin, roast, certification or claim may be invented. It tied each visual decision to a reason and rejected default; named the exact copy reserve; loaded two applicable, medium-salience organizers with verbatim language; declared the quiet families; and specified acceptance, failure, fallback and a one-variable repair. Raw included useful exclusions, but not a dependable lock/repair contract. The skill also prevented me from treating “premium” as a magic word: the material is made visible by the light, instead of being asserted by an adjective.

### 5. Client choice and image verdict

**If I were the client, I would send the merged prompt—not either untouched version.** If forced to send one of the two as written, I would send the skill prompt for a real commercial job because the product/brand boundary and repair contract are safer, but I would keep the raw prompt's opening image and sensory language.

**Which generated the better image in this selected pair? Raw, narrowly.** Its darker cup stays subordinate and the right side has more breathing room for site copy while the copper zipper still catches the eye. The skill-selected image renders more of the cup/bench texture, but that brightness tugs attention toward the copy boundary. This is a one-pair visual judgment only: the candidate systems and user-selected options are confounded, and no image quality or audience claim follows from it.
